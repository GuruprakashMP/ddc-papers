About

DataDrivenChemistryPapers is an open database of research papers that apply machine learning, artificial intelligence or other data-driven methods to chemistry. It holds 42,342 papers and is updated every day.

What is included

A paper is included when a data-driven method is central to it (machine learning, deep learning, language models, chemometrics, Bayesian optimization, data mining, automated or high-throughput experimentation guided by algorithms) and the problem it studies is chemistry: molecules, reactions and synthesis, catalysis, materials chemistry, electrochemistry, polymers, chemical analysis, computational chemistry, medicinal chemistry of small molecules, environmental and chemical engineering. Medicine, biology without small-molecule chemistry, engineering and physics outside chemistry, and papers that mention AI only in passing are not included. Each paper has one chemistry area and the methods it uses; see the categories.

How papers are found

A daily job reads new records from open scholarly metadata services (OpenAlex, Crossref, ChemRxiv, arXiv, Europe PMC, PubMed, Semantic Scholar and DOAJ), removes duplicates and decides whether each paper belongs. Its title or abstract must name a data-driven method; a model trained on papers labelled against the rules above then judges the title, abstract, journal and OpenAlex topic. Like any automatic screen it can miss a paper or let one through. Write to guruprakash29011999@gmail.com to have a record corrected, added or removed.

What is stored

Bibliographic metadata only: title, authors, journal, dates, DOI and categories. Every paper links to its publisher. Abstracts and graphics are not stored: when you press Show abstract, your browser asks OpenAlex or Crossref for it, and graphical abstracts (1,180 papers have one, from Wiley and Elsevier journals) load from the publisher's own server and remain its property.

Privacy

No cookies and no tracking. The papers you mark read or save and the journals you follow stay in your browser's local storage and are never sent anywhere. To count visitors, your browser asks a public counter (abacus.jasoncameron.dev) to add one on your first visit and only reads the total afterwards; that service sees the request, as any website does.
